# AI Search Visibility

### Building B2B brand visibility, representation, and competitive position across search and AI-generated answers

Search is changing.

Buyers increasingly discover, research, compare, and evaluate companies through a combination of traditional search engines and AI-generated answers.

That changes the marketing challenge.

Ranking on a search results page is no longer the only measure of visibility. Brands also need to understand whether they appear in AI-generated answers, **what AI systems say about them, how they are positioned against competitors, and whether that representation reflects how the company wants to be understood.**

I developed an integrated SEO, AEO, and GEO approach designed around that broader objective: **earning visibility and actively managing how the brand is understood wherever buyers seek answers.**

Applied in practice, the approach achieved:

* **Top-position visibility in approximately 75% of targeted AI-generated outputs**
* **Approximately 97% positive brand sentiment in AI-generated responses**
* Results achieved in **under 30 days**

The objective wasn't to optimize for an algorithm.

It was to improve how the market **finds, understands, compares, and evaluates the brand**.

## The Problem

Traditional SEO is built largely around a familiar model:

**Query → Search Results → Website Visit → Conversion**

AI-mediated discovery changes that journey.

A buyer may now ask an AI system:

* What solutions exist for this problem?
* Which vendors should I consider?
* What's the difference between these approaches?
* How does Company A compare with Company B?
* Which solution is better for my use case?
* What are the strengths and weaknesses of each vendor?
* What are the best alternatives?
* What should I know before making a decision?

The AI system can synthesize information from multiple sources, compare companies, make recommendations, and shape the buyer's perception — often before the buyer ever visits a company's website.

That creates two related but different marketing challenges:

**Visibility:** Are we showing up?

**Representation:** When we show up, are we being understood the way we want to be understood?

Marketing therefore needs to answer a broader set of questions:

* Does the brand appear in the answer?
* How prominently does it appear?
* What does the AI say the company does?
* Which capabilities and attributes does it associate with the brand?
* Which category does it place the company in?
* Which competitors does it compare the company against?
* How does it describe those differences?
* When does it recommend the company — and why?
* When does it recommend a competitor instead — and why?
* Does it recognize the company's actual differentiation?
* Is the information accurate and current?
* Is the resulting perception positive, neutral, or negative?
* Is the brand being represented in a way that supports the position the company wants to own?

Traffic and rankings alone cannot answer those questions.

## The Approach

I treat SEO, AEO, and GEO as connected components of a broader **search and AI visibility system**, rather than separate marketing programs.

The approach follows a continuous loop:

**Buyer Questions → Search & AI Landscape → Visibility & Representation → Gaps → Authority & Content Strategy → Execution → Measurement → Refinement**

### 1. Start With Buyer Questions

The starting point isn't a keyword list.

It's understanding what customers and prospects actually need to know throughout the buying journey.

That includes questions around:

* Problems and pain points
* Categories and approaches
* Use cases
* Product capabilities
* Alternatives
* Competitive comparisons
* Implementation
* Pricing and value
* Risk
* Evaluation criteria
* Purchase decisions

These questions create the visibility and perception landscape the brand should understand and influence.

### 2. Establish the Visibility Baseline

Evaluate how the brand currently appears across both traditional search engines and AI-generated answers.

For AI responses, examine:

* Whether the brand appears
* Position or prominence
* Competitors mentioned
* Sources cited
* Topics associated with the brand
* Frequency of appearance
* Sentiment
* Changes across different AI systems

This establishes the first baseline:

**Where are we present, where are we absent, and where are competitors appearing instead?**

### 3. Understand How AI Systems Represent the Brand

Presence alone isn't enough.

A company can appear consistently in AI-generated answers and still be represented in ways that don't support its desired market position.

The next layer of analysis is understanding **what AI systems believe about the company and how they communicate that understanding to buyers.**

This includes evaluating:

* How the company is described
* Which category or categories it is associated with
* Which capabilities and attributes are emphasized
* Which strengths and weaknesses are identified
* Which use cases it is recommended for
* Which competitors it is compared against
* How it performs in head-to-head comparisons
* Why an AI system recommends one vendor over another
* Whether important differentiation is recognized
* Whether outdated or inaccurate perceptions persist
* How sentiment and positioning vary across different LLMs

Competitive prompts are particularly important.

A buyer asking *“What does this company do?”* provides useful information.

But questions such as *“Which solution is better for this use case?”*, *“How does Company A compare with Company B?”*, or *“Which vendors should I consider and why?”* reveal something more strategically valuable:

**how AI systems have constructed the competitive market in the buyer's mind.**

That representation should be compared with the company's intended positioning.

### 4. Identify the Representation Gap

The objective isn't simply to measure what an LLM says.

It's to compare:

**Desired Position → Current LLM Representation → Gap**

For example, a company may want to be understood as an enterprise platform while AI systems consistently describe it as a point solution.

The company may differentiate on one capability while LLMs repeatedly credit a competitor with that advantage.

A strategically important use case may barely appear in AI descriptions.

Or the brand may appear frequently but be recommended for the wrong customer profile.

Those gaps matter because AI-generated answers increasingly become part of the information buyers use to form consideration sets and compare alternatives.

Understanding the gap creates the opportunity to influence it.

### 5. Understand the Information Environment

LLMs don't form brand perceptions in isolation.

Their answers are influenced by the broader information environment surrounding the company, including:

* Company websites
* Product documentation
* Editorial content
* Industry publications
* Review sites
* Community discussions
* Partner ecosystems
* Analyst coverage
* Comparison content
* Customer evidence
* Structured information
* Other authoritative third-party sources

That means AI visibility and representation cannot be solved simply by publishing more blog posts.

When an AI system misunderstands or underrepresents the company, the more useful question is:

**What information is shaping that understanding, and what would need to change for the market and AI systems to understand the company more accurately?**

### 6. Identify Visibility, Authority & Positioning Gaps

Compare the desired market position with what search engines and AI systems currently surface.

Look for gaps such as:

* Important buyer questions where the brand is absent
* Competitors consistently appearing instead
* Incorrect or outdated descriptions
* Weak category association
* Missing differentiation
* Incorrect competitive framing
* Important capabilities that aren't recognized
* Strong attributes disproportionately associated with competitors
* Limited third-party authority
* Important topics without credible supporting content
* Inconsistent language across the information ecosystem

These gaps become strategic priorities.

### 7. Build the Content & Authority Strategy

Once the gap is understood, marketing can determine how to strengthen the information environment around the brand.

Depending on the situation, that can include:

* Clarifying positioning and messaging on owned properties
* Strengthening evidence for differentiated claims
* Foundational category content
* Product and solution pages
* Use-case content
* Technical explanations
* Comparison pages
* Customer evidence
* FAQs
* Original research
* Thought leadership
* Structured data
* Third-party validation
* Community and ecosystem presence
* Correcting inconsistent or outdated information
* Aligning language across product, marketing, documentation, and external sources

The objective isn't content volume.

It's creating a sufficiently clear, consistent, credible, and authoritative body of information for buyers, search engines, and AI systems to understand:

**Who the company is, what it does, where it fits, why it is different, and when someone should choose it.**

### 8. Measure More Than Traffic

A broader visibility model requires broader measurement.

Traditional metrics still matter:

* Organic traffic
* Rankings
* Impressions
* Click-through rates
* Engagement
* Conversion
* Pipeline and revenue contribution

But AI-mediated discovery introduces another measurement layer.

#### Visibility

* AI answer presence
* Position and prominence
* Citation frequency
* Competitive share of voice

#### Representation

* Brand description accuracy
* Category association
* Attribute association
* Differentiation recognition
* Use-case association
* Brand sentiment

#### Competitive Position

* Competitors appearing alongside the brand
* Head-to-head positioning
* Recommendation frequency
* Reasons given for recommendations
* Perceived strengths and weaknesses
* Competitive attributes the LLM associates with each company

Together, these provide a much more complete picture of how the market is discovering and understanding the brand.

## From Monitoring to Influence

Measurement alone isn't the objective.

When a gap exists between **how the company wants to be understood** and **how AI systems currently represent it**, marketing can investigate why and make strategic adjustments.

The loop becomes:

**Desired Position → Current LLM Representation → Gap → Source Analysis → Strategic Adjustment → Re-measurement**

Then repeat.

This isn't about manipulating an individual model or attempting to force a particular answer.

It's about creating a clearer, more consistent, and more authoritative information environment so that AI systems have better evidence from which to understand the company.

That introduces an emerging marketing discipline:

**Managing not only whether the brand is visible to AI, but how the brand is understood, positioned, and compared when AI becomes part of the buyer's decision process.**

## Where AI Changes the Model

AI plays two roles in this system.

First, **AI is changing the buyer's discovery and evaluation experience.**

Second, **AI can dramatically accelerate the marketer's ability to understand and respond to that change.**

AI can help:

* Generate and organize buyer-question landscapes
* Evaluate large sets of AI-generated answers
* Identify patterns in brand representation
* Compare competitive visibility
* Analyze sentiment and positioning
* Identify content and authority gaps
* Examine cited and influential sources
* Monitor changes over time
* Compare responses across multiple LLMs
* Identify inconsistencies in competitive positioning
* Accelerate content research and development
* Surface opportunities for improving authority

The advantage isn't simply producing content faster.

It's creating a much tighter loop between:

**what buyers are asking → what AI systems are telling them → how that compares with the intended position → what marketing needs to change.**

## Human Judgment Is Part of the System

AI visibility creates a temptation to optimize mechanically for AI systems.

I don't think that's the objective.

Marketing still needs to determine:

* Which buyer questions actually matter
* Which categories the company should compete in
* What position the company wants to own
* Which competitive comparisons are strategically important
* What claims are credible
* Which content deserves investment
* Which third-party sources matter
* Whether AI-generated perceptions reflect the company's actual value
* Which representation gaps are strategically meaningful enough to address

AI can reveal how the information environment is interpreting the brand.

Human judgment determines **what the brand wants that understanding to become**.

## Business Impact

Applied in practice, this approach produced measurable change in AI-generated visibility and brand perception in under 60 days.

### ~75% Top-Position Visibility

The brand achieved the top position in approximately 75% of targeted AI-generated outputs.

### ~97% Positive Sentiment

Approximately 97% of measured AI-generated brand references reflected positive sentiment.

Those results demonstrated that AI visibility and representation can be **measured, influenced, and improved** rather than treated as unpredictable side effects of traditional SEO.

More importantly, the work created a framework for continuously understanding how the brand is represented as AI-mediated discovery evolves.

## The Feedback Loop

Search and AI visibility aren't one-time optimization projects.

Buyer behavior changes. Competitors change. Products change. Positioning changes. AI models change. Sources change.

The system therefore operates as a continuous loop:

**Buyer Questions → Visibility & Representation → Competitive Interpretation → Strategic Action → Measurement → New Understanding**

That feedback makes visibility and representation an ongoing market-intelligence capability rather than a periodic SEO exercise.

## Design Principle

The underlying principle is simple:

**Optimize for buyer understanding, not an individual algorithm.**

SEO, AEO, GEO, AI platforms, and discovery interfaces will continue to evolve.

But the strategic objective remains relatively stable:

**When a buyer asks an important question, is the company present, accurately understood, credibly differentiated, and positioned against competitors in a way that reflects the value it wants to own?**
